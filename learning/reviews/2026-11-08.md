## Candidates and decisions

### OGM-20261108-01 — Separate assisted performance from unaided capability

- **Claim / testable hypothesis:** When the human's aim includes learning a skill or exercising independent judgment, an assistant can appear effective during a task while leaving a weaker ability to perform a related task without it. OG-MAT evaluation should **measure these outcomes separately**, not assume one stands in for the other.
- **Origin:** PNAS 2025 mathematics field experiment, considered with the 2024 Tutor CoPilot study and CHI 2025 survey. No private user observation used.
- **Map relationships:** **tests / refines** P-03 (human agency), **extends measurement of** P-07 (useful progress); proposed P-08 (unaided capability) is a *candidate*, not an adopted principle. P-05 (feedback-driven growth) might eventually use these measures.
- **Strength / uncertainty:** direct experimental evidence in a particular learning setting; observational and different intervention evidence offers context. Generalization to professional and creative tasks, prompt-only OG-MAT effects, and durable skill acquisition is **unproven**.
- **Alternative interpretations:** task-specific reliance could account for observed impairment; assisted performance may be the right objective when the human wants execution rather than training; some well-designed assistance improves learning.
- **Proposed disposition:** **test**. Add an optional, low-stakes *unaided-capability follow-up* to [EXPERIMENTS.md](../../EXPERIMENTS.md) and mark P-08 **proposed** in [LEARNING_MAP.md](../../LEARNING_MAP.md). Do **not** force all seed users to work through hints or quizzes.
- **Falsification / reassessment:** controlled or cautiously matched trials across goal types and model families could find no gap, or a gain, between supported tasks and comparable independent follow-ups. Examine whether results depend on task difficulty, familiarity, self-selection, time gap, and whether learning was the user's actual goal.
- **Benefit / downside:** could distinguish genuine skill-building from impressive-looking answers; downsides include test burden, overly pedagogical responses, and mistaken causal claims. Hence optional, consent-based, non-sensitive checks only.

### OGM-20261108-02 — Sycophancy risk involves downstream judgments, not just answer quality

- **Claim / testable hypothesis:** An assistant may be preferred and trusted while affirming a user's weak interpretation in ways that harm their ability to evaluate another person's perspective or consider appropriate repair. A good evaluation must ask more than whether the conversation felt supportive.
- **Origin:** Cheng et al. *Science* 2026 experimental study, independently examined alongside the existing OG-MAT no-sycophancy commitments. The CHI survey provides related but non-causal concerns about AI trust.
- **Map relationships:** **supports concern motivating** P-01 (truth over agreement), **implicates** P-03 (agency) and P-07 (outcome evaluation), **tests** possible divergence between preference and benefit.
- **Strength / uncertainty:** direct human-subject experiments in interpersonal-conflict contexts; there is no measured OG-MAT intervention effect. Contexts outside social dilemmas, long-term effects, and the best corrective style remain uncertain.
- **Alternative interpretations:** empathy and warranted agreement may be beneficial; calling every validating response sycophancy would be an error; indiscriminate challenge could cause social harm.
- **Proposed disposition:** **test**, via a *fictional-perspective / repair* check in [EXPERIMENTS.md](../../EXPERIMENTS.md). Avoid arbitrary disagreement and avoid publishing anyone's private conflict.
- **Falsification / reassessment:** matched response comparisons may show no gains, or worse outcomes, in recognizing credible alternate perspectives and constructive next steps. Rate false accusations and needless contrarianism alongside false validation.
- **Benefit / downside:** an outcome-focused check may expose hidden harms; risks include moralizing, ungrounded blame, and over-weighting an opposing case. The evaluation explicitly requires grounded nuance.

### OGM-20261108-03 — Force Socratic coaching on every task (rejected)

- **Claim considered:** Require assistants to withhold answers and ask guiding questions by default to prevent dependence.
- **Origin:** Overextension of PNAS and Tutor CoPilot pedagogy findings, **not a direct study conclusion**.
- **Map:** would constrain P-03 agency and P-07 useful progress, potentially conflicting with the seed's stated faithfulness to user goals.
- **Evidence / counterevidence:** classroom tutoring benefits from suitable scaffolds, but those findings do **not** imply answer withholding helps users who want a finished document, timely action, or a straightforward fact.
- **Disposition:** **reject now**. Keep [SEED.md](../../SEED.md) unchanged and preserve a goal-sensitive choice between teaching and execution. A future experiment may revisit more contextual guidance.
- **Reassessment:** independently replicated gains for appropriately selected, non-educational contexts without loss of autonomy or usefulness could justify a narrow change.
