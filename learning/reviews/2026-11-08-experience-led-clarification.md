## What we noticed

An initial weekly cultivation review was framed as proactively finding external studies and designing experiments to assess whether their findings could improve OG-MAT. That is one legitimate discovery method, but it differs from the collaboration process the project aims to preserve.

The intended source of weekly cultivation is the **preceding week's real collaboration**: spontaneous curiosity, questions asked during work, the human's corrections, practical failures, productive disagreement, and informal experiments that emerged without a standing research agenda. External evidence has historically been useful when curiosity or an encountered problem made it relevant; it was not the starting requirement.

## What changes

**Old emphasis:** proactively search outside research for candidates, propose new experiments, and incorporate evidence-derived changes.

**New emphasis:** ask what the assistant actually learned from the week's accessible interactions and whether a safely generalizable, useful principle arose. Search externally **only when** a specific observation merits support, challenge, clarification, or safety checking. Preserve a hypothesis or observation without claiming validation or creating a mandatory formal study.

The new model continues to value contrary evidence, independently checked facts, methodological rigor when appropriate, public privacy, and honest uncertainty. It does **not** prohibit outside knowledge or formal research. It simply refuses to make them the default weekly work product.

## Alternatives considered

- **Keep a research-led weekly scan:** may uncover work the collaborators haven't encountered but risks substantial overhead and drift from the seed's organic origins.
- **Exclude external evidence entirely:** minimizes effort but risks insularity and confirmation bias; **not recommended**.
- **Experience-led reflection with targeted external verification:** preserves spontaneous learning while allowing proportional skepticism and outside challenge; **proposed**.

## How to judge the revised process

No formal experiment is commissioned. Over the coming weeks, check whether reviews faithfully identify genuine experience-derived lessons, report their access limitations, distinguish transferable ideas from personal preferences, preserve productive no-change weeks, and avoid made-up memories or unnecessary research activity.

This is a **process preference and a corrective interpretation**, not evidence that experience-led methods universally outperform research-led ones.
