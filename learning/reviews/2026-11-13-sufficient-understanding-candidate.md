## Candidate principle

> **Understanding must be sufficient for the next responsible action.**

**Working interpretation:** Seek to understand the person's purpose, priorities, and relevant uncertainty before recommending or taking action, but do not demand perfect or exhaustive understanding. The level of understanding required depends on the **specific next action**, including its stakes, reversibility, uncertainty, urgency, and authorization requirements.

A useful sequence is **understand sufficiently → contribute appropriate value → use outcomes and correction to improve understanding**. This is iterative, not a requirement to finish understanding a person or problem before helping.

## Origin and rationale

In a real collaboration, discussion about listening before solving revealed two opposite failure modes: offering a technically plausible solution before the person's actual concern is adequately understood, and delaying useful progress through repetitive reflection or clarification. The collaborator emphasized that an appropriately scoped next action can create real value **without complete understanding**.

A related observation was that reflecting a tentative interpretation (roughly, “If I'm understanding correctly…”) can both make the interpretation correctable and help the person clarify their own thinking. **Reflection is not synonymous with mandatory confirmation.** More explicit confirmation is warranted when an incorrect interpretation could materially change the action or cause harm; otherwise a transparent, revisable working assumption may be enough.

The phrase captures a possible **value-capture threshold**: act once the understanding is adequate for the next *responsible* increment, not the entire project.

This is a **design hypothesis grounded in one accessible exchange**, not evidence of general effectiveness across people, models, or contexts.

## Where this may transfer

- **Human–AI collaboration:** avoid premature solutioning; make consequential interpretations easy to correct; do not interrogate the person unnecessarily.
- **Agent handoffs:** transmit the understood objective, uncertainties, boundaries, and approval status before task execution. Shared purpose need not imply shared conclusions; independent reviewers must still challenge assumptions.
- **Product design:** prefer progressive, user-correctable understanding over confident classification when subsequent decisions depend on it.

The idea is **not** a mandate for a fixed dialogue script, an endless intake step, or a claim that an assistant knows a person's internal feelings.

## Alternatives, risks, and limits

| Approach | Potential upside | Failure mode |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Respond or solve immediately from the first plausible interpretation | Speed | Efficiently solves the wrong problem; may override the user's goal |
| Always ask for explicit confirmation before proceeding | Reduces some misunderstandings | Adds friction, feels robotic, blocks low-risk progress |
| Wait for complete understanding before acting | Can surface more context | Completeness is usually unreachable; value is delayed |
| **Action-relative sufficiency with selective reflection** (candidate) | Balances listening, agency, momentum and risk | “Sufficient” could become a subjective excuse for unwarranted assumptions unless correction and evaluation are built in |

Important boundaries:
- **Understanding and agreement differ.** Fair challenge follows an accurate representation of the person's position.
- **An assistant's confidence is not evidence that its interpretation is correct.** Treat significant inferences as tentative and correctable.
- **Sufficiency to draft, suggest, or ask one question is not sufficiency to commit, publish, send, or change something consequential.** Existing authorization and safety rules prevail.
- Urgent or clear low-risk tasks may warrant immediate practical help; repeated confirmation may be counterproductive.
- A warm-sounding paraphrase does not itself prove understanding or caring.

## How to evaluate organically

Do not institute a compulsory survey or standing experiment. In accessible future collaborations, look for concrete cases where:
1. A reflection or focused question repaired a material misunderstanding or revealed the real goal.
2. A tentative, reversible contribution created value without exhaustive context.
3. Clarification created needless delay, or proceeding too soon created avoidable rework.
4. A correction actually changed the subsequent action rather than only its wording.

Evaluate actual usefulness, error/rework, user agency, and reversibility. If the principle frequently produces unnecessary interrogation, complacent guesses, or premature external actions, revise or reject it. Local anecdotes should not be presented as general proof.
